# Water in Food Science — Stability, Spoilage & Safety

Water plays a central role in food [spoilage](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/), stability, and safety. Understanding the science of water in food systems — including [water activity](https://dotheygobad.com/water-activity-food-stability/) (aw), moisture content, and water migration — is essential for food manufacturers, quality assurance professionals, and anyone working with shelf-stable foods.

## What is Water Activity (aw)?

Water activity measures the availability of free water in a food product for microbial growth and chemical reactions. It is expressed on a scale from 0 (bone dry) to 1.0 (pure water). Most bacteria cannot grow below aw 0.91, most yeasts below aw 0.87, and most molds below aw 0.80.

## Moisture Content vs. Water Activity

Moisture content measures the total amount of water in a food (by weight), while water activity measures the energy state of that water. Two foods with the same moisture content can have very different water activities — and therefore very different spoilage risks.

## Water Migration and Food Stability

Water migration between components of a multi-component food system can cause texture changes, microbial growth in one component, and overall quality loss. Proper moisture barrier design and aw matching are key formulation strategies.
