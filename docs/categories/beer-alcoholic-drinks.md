# Beer & Alcoholic Drinks

Ethanol is a preservative, which is why beer, wine and spirits are among the few food categories where the question is not "will it grow mould" but "will it still taste right". The failure modes are chemical: oxygen ingress, light-driven reactions, ester hydrolysis, and the slow staling of hop compounds.

The numbers that matter differ by product. Beer is judged in weeks to months because hop aroma is fragile and dissolved oxygen in the package is measured in parts per billion. Wine evolves over years, and its main enemies are oxygen and temperature swings rather than microbes. Spirits at 40% ABV are microbiologically stable indefinitely; what changes is the bottle, the closure and the light exposure.

This category covers the chemistry behind those timelines — why a beer goes skunky, why wine faults smell of wet cardboard or vinegar, and what packaging choices actually extend shelf life.

## What drives shelf life here

- **Dissolved oxygen** in the package drives staling; every 100 ppb shortens flavour life measurably.
- **Light** at 550 nm converts hop iso-alpha acids into 3-MBT, the "skunky" compound — brown glass and cans block it.
- **Temperature** accelerates ester hydrolysis and Maillard reactions; a cellar at 12 °C is not the same product as a shelf at 25 °C.
- **Acetic acid bacteria** need oxygen; a sealed, sulfited wine is protected, an open bottle is not.
