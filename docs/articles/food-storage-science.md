## TL;DR 

 Three temperature zones govern food storage: refrigerator (0-4 °C), freezer (-18 °C), and room temperature (18-22 °C). Each zone serves a different preservation function. Refrigeration slows [microbial](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) and enzymatic activity; freezing stops [microbial](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) growth entirely; room temperature relies on low [water activity](https://dotheygobad.com/water-activity-food-stability/) or high acidity for stability. Proper packaging is as important as temperature control. 

## What Foods Should Be Refrigerated? 

 
 
 
 
 Food Category 
 Refrigerate? 
 Why or Why Not 
 
 
 
 
 Fresh dairy (milk, yogurt, cheese) 
 ✅ Yes 
 Psychrotrophic bacteria grow at room temp 
 
 
 Fresh meat and poultry 
 ✅ Yes 
 Pseudomonas is the dominant [spoilage](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) bacteria 
 
 
 Eggs (US) 
 ✅ Yes 
 Cuticle washed off; refrigeration prevents Salmonella 
 
 
 Eggs (EU) 
 ❌ No need 
 Cuticle intact; natural antimicrobial barrier 
 
 
 Bread 
 ❌ No 
 Starch retrogradation accelerates at 0-7 °C 
 
 
 Tomatoes 
 ❌ No 
 Chilling injury above 10 °C destroys flavor 
 
 
 Potatoes 
 ❌ No 
 Cold converts starch to sugar; alters texture 
 
 
 Olive oil 
 ❌ No 
 Cold causes condensation and turbidity 
 
 
 Honey 
 ❌ No 
 Crystallization accelerates; osmotic preservation works at any temp 
 
 
 
 

## Freezer Storage Best Practices 

 
 Maintain -18 °C (0 °F) or colder at all times 
 Use moisture-proof packaging to prevent freezer burn 
 Remove as much air as possible before sealing 
 Label with date and contents; frozen food quality degrades over time 
 Freeze in portion sizes to avoid repeated thawing 
 Do not refreeze thawed raw meat 
 

## Key Takeaways 

 
 Refrigeration at 4 °C doubles or triples shelf life vs room temperature 
 Some foods (bread, tomatoes, potatoes) are damaged by refrigeration 
 Freezer burn is dehydration + oxidation, not [microbial](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) [spoilage](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) 
 Packaging choice (vacuum, MAP, moisture barrier) directly affects shelf life 
 Humidity control in storage prevents both drying and condensation
