# Categories

The library is organised by product category because spoilage mechanisms cluster that way. Dairy fails through enzymatic breakdown and psychrotrophic growth; produce fails through respiration, ethylene and fungal infection; condiments fail when a preservation hurdle is quietly removed. Reading within a category shows the same variables recurring, which is how the underlying science becomes useful rather than abstract.

Eight categories, 114 articles:

- [Beer & Alcoholic Drinks](beer-alcoholic-drinks.md) — oxidation, light damage, packaging oxygen
- [Dairy Products](dairy-products.md) — psychrotrophs, lipolysis, water activity in cheese
- [Fruits & Vegetables](fruits-vegetables.md) — respiration, ethylene, chilling injury
- [Grains & Baked Goods](grains-baked-goods.md) — staling, rancidity, mould
- [Meat & Seafood](meat-seafood.md) — bacterial growth, histamine, cold chain
- [Non-Alcoholic Beverages](non-alcoholic-beverages.md) — pH, preservatives, thermal processing
- [Sauces & Condiments](sauces-condiments.md) — water activity, emulsions, osmophilic yeasts
- [Food Industry Concepts](food-industry-concept.md) — the principles behind all of the above

If you are not sure where to start, [Food Industry Concepts](food-industry-concept.md) explains water activity, pH and hurdle technology — the three variables that decide almost every shelf-life question on this site.
