# Grains & Baked Goods

Grains are dry, which makes them stable for months — until water, heat or time changes the picture. Flour and rice fail through lipid oxidation and insects. Bread fails through starch retrogradation within days and mould within a week. Pasta is stable until moisture migrates in, and whole-wheat products go rancid faster than white because the germ oil is still present.

The distinction between staling and spoilage matters commercially. A stale loaf is safe but unsaleable; a mouldy loaf is a waste issue and, for some moulds, a mycotoxin question. Both are driven by water: retrogradation needs mobile water to reorganise amylopectin, and mould needs a_w above roughly 0.80.

These articles separate the chemistry from the microbiology so that a shelf-life extension decision — an emulsifier, a modified atmosphere, a different flour — can be matched to the mechanism it actually addresses.

## What drives shelf life here

- **Starch retrogradation** firms the crumb within 24–72 hours; it is reversible with heat, unlike mould.
- **a_w below 0.80** stops mould growth, which is why dry crackers keep and fresh bread does not.
- **Lipid oxidation** in wholemeal flour and brown rice produces rancid off-notes within months.
- ** Moisture migration** between components (filling and crust) is a common cause of premature failure.
