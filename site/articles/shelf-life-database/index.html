    TL;DR Dairy Products Shelf Life Grains and Baked Goods Shelf Life Key Takeaways

    TL;DR

    Food shelf life varies dramatically by category and storage condition. Dairy lasts 1-4 weeks refrigerated; hard grains 1-10 years in dry storage; frozen meat 4-12 months at -18 °C. The single most important factor for extending shelf life is temperature control: every 10 °C reduction halves spoilage rates. This database provides shelf life data for all major food categories with scientific context for why shelf life differs within each group.

    Dairy Products Shelf Life

    Product Refrigerated (4 °C) Frozen (-18 °C) Room Temp Key Spoilage Driver

    Fresh Milk 5-7 days 3 months 2 hours Bacterial growth (psychrotrophs)

    UHT Milk 2-4 weeks (opened) Not recommended 6-9 months (unopened) Lipolysis, proteolysis

    Hard Cheese 2-8 weeks (wrapped) 6 months 1-2 weeks Mold growth, moisture loss

    Soft Cheese 1-2 weeks 2 months 2-4 hours Bacterial spoilage, lipolysis

    Butter (Salted) 3-6 months 9-12 months 1-2 weeks Lipolytic rancidity

    Butter (Unsalted) 1-3 months 6-9 months Fewer days Oxidative rancidity, microbial

    Yogurt 2-4 weeks 2 months 2 hours Post-acidification, whey separation

    Ice Cream N/A 2-4 months 30 min (melted) Recrystallization, fat destabilization

    Grains and Baked Goods Shelf Life

    Product Room Temp Refrigerated Frozen Key Spoilage Driver

    White Rice 2+ years N/A N/A Lipid oxidation, insect infestation

    Brown Rice 6-8 months 1 year 2 years Lipid rancidity (germ oil)

    White Flour 1 year (pantry) 2 years 5 years Insect infestation, rancidity

    Whole Wheat Flour 3-6 months 6-12 months 2 years Germ oil rancidity

    Fresh Bread 3-7 days 7-14 days 3-6 months Starch retrogradation, mold

    Dry Pasta 2-3 years N/A N/A Moisture migration, texture loss

    Key Takeaways

    Dairy spoils fastest: even refrigerated, fresh dairy lasts only 1-2 weeks Dry goods last longest: low aw ( Frozen shelf life is limited by quality (not safety)—enzymes still active below 0 °F Salted butter lasts 2-3× longer than unsalted due to microbial inhibition Whole grains spoil faster than refined grains because the oil-rich germ remains
