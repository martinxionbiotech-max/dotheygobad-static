    TL;DR What Foods Should Be Refrigerated? Freezer Storage Best Practices Key Takeaways

    TL;DR

    Three temperature zones govern food storage: refrigerator (0-4 °C), freezer (-18 °C), and room temperature (18-22 °C). Each zone serves a different preservation function. Refrigeration slows microbial and enzymatic activity; freezing stops microbial growth entirely; room temperature relies on low water activity or high acidity for stability. Proper packaging is as important as temperature control.

    What Foods Should Be Refrigerated?

    Food Category Refrigerate? Why or Why Not

    Fresh dairy (milk, yogurt, cheese) ✅ Yes Psychrotrophic bacteria grow at room temp

    Fresh meat and poultry ✅ Yes Pseudomonas is the dominant spoilage bacteria

    Eggs (US) ✅ Yes Cuticle washed off; refrigeration prevents Salmonella

    Eggs (EU) ❌ No need Cuticle intact; natural antimicrobial barrier

    Bread ❌ No Starch retrogradation accelerates at 0-7 °C

    Tomatoes ❌ No Chilling injury above 10 °C destroys flavor

    Potatoes ❌ No Cold converts starch to sugar; alters texture

    Olive oil ❌ No Cold causes condensation and turbidity

    Honey ❌ No Crystallization accelerates; osmotic preservation works at any temp

    Freezer Storage Best Practices

    Maintain -18 °C (0 °F) or colder at all times Use moisture-proof packaging to prevent freezer burn Remove as much air as possible before sealing Label with date and contents; frozen food quality degrades over time Freeze in portion sizes to avoid repeated thawing Do not refreeze thawed raw meat

    Key Takeaways

    Refrigeration at 4 °C doubles or triples shelf life vs room temperature Some foods (bread, tomatoes, potatoes) are damaged by refrigeration Freezer burn is dehydration + oxidation, not microbial spoilage Packaging choice (vacuum, MAP, moisture barrier) directly affects shelf life Humidity control in storage prevents both drying and condensation
