## Natural vs Stabilized Peanut Butter 

 Natural peanut butter (ground peanuts only) has oil separation as the peanut oil rises to the top. This is normal — the oil is still good but needs stirring. Stabilized peanut butter contains hydrogenated vegetable oil (a [food additive](https://dotheygobad.com/ingredients-additives/)) that keeps the oil emulsified. Stabilized versions also have a longer shelf life because the hydrogenation reduces unsaturated fat content, slowing [oxidation](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/). Both types are safe — the real [spoilage](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) risk is [rancidity](https://dotheygobad.com/does-olive-oil-go-bad) from fat oxidation, indicated by a sharp, paint-like smell. 
 Peanut butter spoils via [oxidation](https://dotheygobad.com/what-makes-food-go-bad/). [Does Peanut Butter Go Bad?](https://dotheygobad.com/does-peanut-butter-go-bad/) 

### Quick Answer: Is Stabilized Peanut Butter Safe? 

 Yes, the hydrogenated vegetable oil in stabilized peanut butter is safe. It&#8217;s a small amount (typically 1–2%) that keeps the peanut oil emulsified so you never have to stir. Both natural and stabilized peanut butter are perfectly safe — choose based on texture preference and how often you use it. 

### The Science Behind Natural Peanut Butter Separation 

 Natural peanut butter is simply ground roasted peanuts. Peanut oil is approximately 50% monounsaturated (oleic acid) and 30% polyunsaturated (linoleic acid) — both liquid at room temperature. The solid peanut solids (protein, fiber, starch) are denser than the oil, so gravity naturally separates them over time. This is not [spoilage](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) — it&#8217;s a physical process called sedimentation. The separated oil is still fresh unless exposed to light, heat, and oxygen for extended periods. 
 Stabilized peanut butter uses a small amount of fully hydrogenated vegetable oil (often rapeseed or soybean oil) which is solid at room temperature. This creates a crystalline fat network that traps the liquid peanut oil in a stable emulsion. Hydrogenation saturates the double bonds in the oil, making it resistant to oxidation — but the amount is so small that it has minimal impact on overall nutrition. ### How to Choose and Store Peanut Butter 

 
 Natural peanut butter: Best for short-term use (2–3 months). Store upside down in the pantry to redistribute oil naturally, or stir thoroughly after opening and refrigerate to prevent reseparation. Refrigeration slows oxidation but makes the butter harder to spread. 
 Stabilized peanut butter: Better for long-term storage (6–12 months after opening). No stirring needed. Keep refrigerated for maximum shelf life, though room temperature is fine for daily use. Learn more about [spoilage](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) factors in [what makes food go bad](https://dotheygobad.com/what-makes-food-go-bad/). 
 Signs of [spoilage](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/): Both types eventually go rancid. If peanut butter smells like paint, putty, or has a sharp chemical odor, it&#8217;s oxidized and should be discarded regardless of type. 
 

### Bottom Line on Natural vs Stabilized 

 Both are safe and shelf-stable — peanut butter&#8217;s low [water activity](https://dotheygobad.com/what-is-water-activity-aw/) (0.3–0.5) prevents all [microbial](https://dotheygobad.com/microbial-vs-chemical-spoilage-explained/) growth. The choice between natural and stabilized comes down to convenience: stabilized wins for no-stir ease, natural wins for purity and texture preference.
